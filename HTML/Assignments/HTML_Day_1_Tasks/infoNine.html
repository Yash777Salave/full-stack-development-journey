Bajirao Peshwa

Information

Bajirao I (born Visaji,18 August 1700 – 28 April 1740 ) was the 7th Peshwa of the Maratha Empire. Born in a Brahmin family as the son of Peshwa Balaji Vishwanath, he began his military career at a young age. After the death of Balaji, he was proclaimed Peshwa and over the new two decades he led the Maratha army on an unprecedented campaign of expansion, conquering Mughal territory up till Delhi and Cuttack.
He further solidified Maratha authority in the Deccan by defeating the Nizam of Hyderabad at Palkhed and liberated several kingdoms and vassals from Mughal influence. Among them was the Bundela ruler Chhatrasal, whom Bajirao rescued from a Mughal siege, gaining independence for Bundelkhand. Subsequently, Chhatrasal granted Bajirao a jagir and his daughter Mastani's hand in marriage.
Bajirao asserted Maratha tax rights in Gujarat, defeating rebel Trimbak Rao Dabhade in 1731 at Dabhoi; he also engaged in a diplomatic mission to persuade Rajput courts for chauth payments. Further efforts to establish Maratha dominance saw him responsible for the Battle of Delhi (1737) which may be said to mark the pinnacle of his military career.
He secured the important territory of Malwa after defeating the combined forces of Mughal-Nizam-Nawab of Awadh in Battle of Bhopal (1737). At the time of his death in 1740, the Maratha Empire had reached its greatest territorial extent yet and was amongst the largest powers of the subcontinent. Bajirao's adventurous life has been picturized in Indian cinema and also featured in novels. Bajirao had two wives: Kashibai and Mastani.
Bajirao's relationship with his second wife Mastani is a controversial subject; very little is known with certainty about it. She was generally referenced cryptically in books, letters or documents from that era.
